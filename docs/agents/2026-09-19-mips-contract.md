## Design and scope

- Visual thesis: the existing graphite-and-bone atlas workbench, with a large reference brain and a restrained caption column.
- Content: Orient, Compare, Explain; source-linked framework and a short evidence panel; fictional target and abstract entry points explicitly identified.
- Interaction: rotating anatomy, changing named camera views, switching A/B/both corridor volumes, and comparing two illustrative widths. Reduced motion remains respected. No ornamental animation.
- New standalone page, small pure corridor model and rendering overlay, controller/CSS, direct links from landing and Case Conference, public export/route/sitemap integration, focused tests and CI.
- Reuse the existing pinned atlas renderer and data. Both volumes terminate at the same authored target. No patient imaging, new dependency, paid service, credentials or global configuration.
- Notes are session-only with an optional text download; no changes to existing lesson/case progress. No corridor score, preferred operative route, deformation simulation, patient registration or claim of clinical validation.
- Short educational wording must separate visible reference geometry from unrendered vessels, short association fibres, patient-specific displacement and intraoperative evidence.
