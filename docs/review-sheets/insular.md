## Steps (debrief entries)

### Debrief 0: Treat an absent layer as missing evidence

**Claim:** The source display never contained the vascular relationship under discussion. A visible gap between sampled tracts therefore cannot answer the vessel question. The new report identifies uncertainty; it does not prove encasement or a particular operative endpoint.

**Sources:** NO SOURCES

### Debrief 1: Connect the anatomical and clinical evidence

**Claim:** Microsurgical studies describe insular arterial relationships. A clinical insular-glioma series specifically examined tumor position relative to lenticulostriate arteries. These are reasons to discuss vascular anatomy independently of tract display; neither makes this reference atlas a patient-specific margin map.

**Sources:**
- **insula-anatomy** — Tanriover et al. · Microsurgical anatomy of the insula and sylvian fissure (PMID 15137609)
- **lsa** — Resection of insular gliomas: importance of lenticulostriate artery position (PMID 18976071)

### Debrief 2: Make the request concrete

**Claim:** For this exercise, identify which individual imaging, functional assessment and vessel relationships are missing. Keep cortical, white-matter and vascular evidence distinct. A well-calibrated response can retain the anatomical hypothesis while withdrawing an unsupported safety conclusion.

**Sources:** NO SOURCES

### Debrief 3: A defensible response

**Claim:** “The apparent tract gap is a property of the reconstruction shown. The medial vessel relationship is unresolved, so I cannot translate that gap into a margin. I would explicitly ask the team to characterize the missing vascular and deep anatomical relationships and correlate them with the wider functional assessment.”
