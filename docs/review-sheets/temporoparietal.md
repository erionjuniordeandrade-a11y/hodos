## Steps (debrief entries)

### Debrief 0: Replace the global label with the measured tasks

**Claim:** Fluent conversation and performance on a structured repetition task are different observations. The new information makes the phrase “language intact” insufficiently specific. It does not establish that any other language function is normal.

**Sources:** NO SOURCES

### Debrief 1: Use a pathway hypothesis, not a tract diagnosis

**Claim:** Saur and colleagues combined task fMRI and tractography to investigate repetition and comprehension routes. Their task-based dorsal/ventral account offers a framework for discussion. It does not turn this fictional task profile into proof of an isolated fascicle lesion.

**Sources:**
- **language** — Saur et al. · Ventral and dorsal pathways for language (PMID 19004769)

### Debrief 2: Keep vision as an independent unanswered question

**Claim:** Work on temporal-resection cohorts combined tractography with visual-field outcomes and described variation in Meyer’s loop. It supports keeping visual function and individual anatomy in the assessment, not transferring a temporal-surgery risk estimate to this fictional temporoparietal case.

**Sources:**
- **vision** — Yogarajah et al. · Defining Meyer’s loop: temporal-lobe resections, visual fields and tractography (PMID 19460796)

### Debrief 3: A defensible response

**Claim:** “Conversation was fluent, but the added testing identifies a task-specific difficulty that deserves a more precise language description. I would use dorsal and ventral references to frame hypotheses, correlate them with individual evidence, and keep the unassessed visual field explicit rather than inferring it from the schematic.”

**Sources:** NO SOURCES
