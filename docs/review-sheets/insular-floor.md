# The insular floor

Lesson id: `insular-floor` · Minutes: 14 · Review status: draft

**Summary:** Orient the buried insular surface, compare its ventral and upper relationships, and distinguish sampled pathways from the absent layers and vessels beneath it.

**Goals:**
  - Distinguish gross insular landmarks from AVI and PoI2 parcel references.
  - Compare population bundle families around the insula without treating them as individual boundaries.
  - Name the deep grey references and the unrendered layers and vessels.

**Audience:** Neurosurgical residents

## Opening question

Which parts of the insular floor can the atlas show, and which must remain anatomical context?

## Three takeaways

1. A limited Sylvian opening may leave superior and posterior insula concealed; AVI and PoI2 do not draw those hidden limits. _(step 0: Look beyond the exposed Sylvian band)_
2. The superior limiting sulcus is anatomical context; the displayed CST does not mark its boundary. _(step 3: Look deep to the superior limiting sulcus)_
3. Lateral lenticulostriate arteries usually arise from M1, whereas insular perforators arise from M2; neither system is rendered. _(step 5: Compare M1 and M2 perforator origins)_

## Steps

### Step 0: Look beyond the exposed Sylvian band

**Claim:** A limited Sylvian opening may expose only a band of insula, leaving superior and posterior portions concealed beneath the opercula. The highlighted parcels do not delineate those hidden limits.

**Observe:** Select AVI and PoI2, lower surface opacity, then compare left and superior views. Use the parcel labels to orient the buried surface, and note that the view does not show a Sylvian opening or draw the concealed limits.

**Anatomy:**
  - A limited Sylvian opening may expose only a band of insula.
  - Superior and posterior portions may remain concealed beneath the opercula.
  - A parcel highlight does not delineate those concealed limits.

**Surgical:** For resident teaching, describe the exposed band separately from the superior and posterior insula hidden beneath the opercula. The population parcel highlights help orient the surface but do not define the concealed extent.

**Question:** When a limited Sylvian opening exposes a band, which insular portions may remain concealed beneath the opercula?

**Answer:** Superior and posterior portions may remain concealed. The parcel highlights do not delineate those hidden limits.

### Step 1: Follow the inferior limit to the limen

**Claim:** The inferior limiting sulcus approaches the limen at the ventral insular transition. IFOF and UF are population bundle samples associated with ventral and anterior insular relationships; they do not draw the sulcus or define the limen as a fibre endpoint. The scene contains the pathway references, not the named sulcus or limen itself.

**Observe:** Show IFOF and UF together, then rotate from lateral to anterior. Describe the inferior sulcus and limen from the anatomical account, and identify the two highlighted objects as bundle samples.

**Anatomy:**
  - Microsurgical anatomy describes the inferior limiting sulcus and temporal-stem relationship.
  - The limen is a gross landmark at the ventral insular transition.
  - IFOF and UF are pathway-family references, not capsule-layer meshes.
  - The rendered bundles do not establish an individual sulcus, limen or endpoint.

**Surgical:** When presenting this relationship, name the limen and inferior limiting sulcus from anatomy, then describe the pathway samples separately.

**Question:** Do the IFOF and UF highlights mark where the inferior limiting sulcus ends?

**Answer:** No. They are bundle references, whereas the limiting sulcus and limen are anatomical landmarks. Their proximity in a view does not make a sulcal boundary or a measured endpoint.

## Full source list

- **Q1** — Türe et al., 1999, Topographic anatomy of the insular region. (PMID 10193618) — experimental_anatomy
  - _Scope:_ Human cadaveric anatomy of the insular gyri, external and extreme capsules, claustrum and lentiform relationships. It anchors the layered transsylvian description without supplying a patient-specific corridor.
- **Q4** — Ribas et al., 2015, Microsurgical anatomy of the inferior limiting insular sulcus and the temporal stem. (PMID 25859806) — experimental_anatomy
  - _Scope:_ Human microsurgical anatomy of the inferior limiting insular sulcus and temporal stem. It supports the anatomical boundaries of transsylvian and transcortical descriptions without making a universal distance claim.
- **R5** — Tanriover et al., 2004 · microsurgical anatomy of the insula and Sylvian fissure (PMID 15137609) — experimental_anatomy
  - _Scope:_ Human microsurgical anatomy of insular gyri, the Sylvian fissure and MCA branches/perforators. Those vessels are discussed, not rendered here.
- **M1** — Glasser et al., 2016 · multi-modal parcellation of human cerebral cortex (PMID 27437579) — atlas
  - _Scope:_ HCP population reference built from converging cortical features. A parcel boundary here is a group boundary; it does not locate one person’s functional border and it has not been registered to any individual in this viewer.
- **D1** — Van Essen et al., 2013 · the WU-Minn Human Connectome Project overview (PMID 23684880) — atlas
  - _Scope:_ Provenance for the S1200 group-average surface rendered here. A group-average midthickness surface is a population object; individual gyral and sulcal anatomy differs from it.
- **R3** — Kier et al., 2004 · temporal stem, uncinate, IFOF and optic radiation (PMID 15140705) — experimental_anatomy
  - _Scope:_ Human anatomical dissection correlated with MR imaging. Supports relative temporal-stem anatomy; not a universal surgical distance.
- **D2** — Yeh, 2022 · population-based tract-to-region connectome of the human brain (PMID 35995773) — atlas
  - _Scope:_ Provenance for the HCP1065 population-averaged bundles rendered here. Every displayed line is an average-space reconstruction sampled for display; it is not a patient’s pathway and carries no direction of conduction.
- **K3** — Fernández-Miranda et al., 2008, three-dimensional microsurgical and tractographic anatomy of cerebral white matter (PMID 18695585) — experimental_anatomy
  - _Scope:_ Human fibre dissection and tractographic anatomy of the cingulum and its relationship to the corpus callosum and cingulate gyrus.
- **R2** — Ribas et al., 2018 · microsurgical anatomy of the central core (PMID 29271710) — experimental_anatomy
  - _Scope:_ Stepwise human dissections of the insula, capsules, basal ganglia and thalamus. Gross atlas meshes illustrate only part of these layered relationships.
- **Q5** — Costa et al., 2018, A Technical Guide for Fiber Tract Dissection of the Internal Capsule. (PMID 29465740) — experimental_anatomy
  - _Scope:_ A stepwise Klingler dissection guide to the anterior limb, genu, posterior limb, retrolenticular and sublenticular relationships. It supports a teaching description of relative organisation while leaving method and level limits explicit.
- **D3** — Tian et al., 2020 · topographic organization of the human subcortex (PMID 32989295) — atlas
  - _Scope:_ Provenance for the Melbourne Subcortex Atlas scale-1 meshes rendered here. Scale 1 is the coarsest level: anterior and posterior thalamus are merged and no brainstem nucleus is included.
