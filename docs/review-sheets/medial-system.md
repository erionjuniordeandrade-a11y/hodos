# The medial system: fornix, cingulum and the Papez loop

Lesson id: `medial-system` · Minutes: 16 · Review status: draft

**Summary:** Compare the fornix and cingulum as distinct medial pathways, then use their place in the Papez model to qualify what a population atlas can establish.

**Goals:**
  - Trace the fornix waypoints while identifying structures absent from the scene.
  - Compare cingulum families as population-level reconstructed routes.
  - Distinguish a historical circuit model from evidence about individual memory function.

**Audience:** Neurosurgical residents

## Opening question

How do the fornix and cingulum enter the Papez model, and what does the atlas leave unresolved?

## Three takeaways

1. Name the fornix waypoints from anatomical sources, not from the F render alone. _(step 0: Trace the fornix through its named waypoints)_
2. Compare cingulum families as reconstructed routes rather than histological compartments. _(step 2: Compare the cingulum route families)_
3. Identify the mammillary bodies, mammillothalamic tract and anterior thalamic nuclei as links absent from the displayed route. _(step 5: Bound what a highlighted route says about memory)_

**Observe:** Show all five cingulum families in the medial view. Compare their broad courses with the selected cortical references, without treating a parcel outline as a tract endpoint.

**Anatomy:**

### Step 4: Keep the fornix and cingulum distinct in a limbic account

**Claim:** The fornix and cingulum appear together in classic limbic accounts, but they have distinct courses: F is a hippocampal output family, while the cingulum contains medial association routes. Showing them together does not make them one continuous cable or demonstrate fibres between the families.

**Observe:** Show F with C_FPH and C_PH, then compare each route with the gross hippocampal reference. Name the source that supports each anatomical description before opening the answer.

**Anatomy:**
  - F and the cingulum are separate installed pathway families.
  - The fornix anatomy and cingulum subdivisions come from different anatomical and reconstruction evidence.
  - The hippocampus is shown at gross scale; the mammillary bodies and fine cingulum endpoints are absent.
  - A single view cannot establish which fibres are intact in an individual.

**Surgical:** A resident explanation should name the pathway under discussion before attaching it to a broader memory model. This group render does not show whether either route is intact in a person.

**Question:** If both pathways appear in one limbic model, are they one tract?

**Answer:** No. F and the cingulum are distinct anatomical systems that can be placed in a broader circuit model. The scene shows their population geometry and does not establish a continuous fibre connection between them.

## Full source list

- **MS2** — Senova et al., 2020 · fornix anatomy and function (PMID 32132227) — conceptual_model
  - _Scope:_ This review summarises fornix anatomy and functional evidence. It does not establish an individual memory outcome or a surgical boundary.
- **K5** — Güngör et al., 2017, white matter tracts in ventricular surgery and hydrocephalus (PMID 27257832) — experimental_anatomy
  - _Scope:_ Rhoton lineage fibre dissection relating callosal and deep white matter structures to the transcallosal ventricular corridor. This is anatomical context, not operative instruction.
- **D2** — Yeh, 2022 · population-based tract-to-region connectome of the human brain (PMID 35995773) — atlas
  - _Scope:_ Provenance for the HCP1065 population-averaged bundles rendered here. Every displayed line is an average-space reconstruction sampled for display; it is not a patient’s pathway and carries no direction of conduction.
- **D3** — Tian et al., 2020 · topographic organization of the human subcortex (PMID 32989295) — atlas
  - _Scope:_ Provenance for the Melbourne Subcortex Atlas scale-1 meshes rendered here. Scale 1 is the coarsest level: anterior and posterior thalamus are merged and no brainstem nucleus is included.
- **MS3** — Bubb et al., 2018 · cingulum anatomy, function and dysfunction (PMID 29753752) — conceptual_model
  - _Scope:_ This review synthesises cingulum anatomy and reported functions. It does not support a segment-to-symptom rule or an individual prediction.
- **MS4** — Catani et al., 2013 · revised limbic-system model (PMID 23850593) — conceptual_model
  - _Scope:_ This review proposes a revised model of limbic relationships across memory, emotion and behaviour. It supports treating Papez as one historical framework, not as a complete circuit or a single-function map.
- **MS1** — Jones et al., 2013 · cingulum subdivisions by diffusion MRI fibre tracking (PMID 23178227) — reconstruction
  - _Scope:_ Diffusion MRI fibre tracking describes subdivisions within the cingulum bundle. It supports a model-based account of regional courses, not histological boundaries or an individual endpoint map.
- **K3** — Fernández-Miranda et al., 2008, three-dimensional microsurgical and tractographic anatomy of cerebral white matter (PMID 18695585) — experimental_anatomy
  - _Scope:_ Human fibre dissection and tractographic anatomy of the cingulum and its relationship to the corpus callosum and cingulate gyrus.
- **M1** — Glasser et al., 2016 · multi-modal parcellation of human cerebral cortex (PMID 27437579) — atlas
  - _Scope:_ HCP population reference built from converging cortical features. A parcel boundary here is a group boundary; it does not locate one person’s functional border and it has not been registered to any individual in this viewer.
