# Corpus callosum & the interhemispheric corridor

Lesson id: `corpus-callosum` · Minutes: 16 · Review status: draft

## Opening question

Which callosal segment and neighbouring systems matter when a posterior disconnection hypothesis affects visual transfer but spares basic motor output?

## Three takeaways

1. Orient callosal segments with the cingulate gyrus and cingulum on the medial surface. _(step 0: Read the callosal arc on the medial surface)_
2. Relate the anterior body to medial frontal parcels, FAT and the initiation question. _(step 2: Place the SMA and frontal aslant relationship)_
3. Use splenial topography to separate visual transfer from the descending motor pathway. _(step 6: Explain why splenial disconnection can spare motor output)_

## Steps

### Step 0: Read the callosal arc on the medial surface

**Claim:** Start with the medial surface as a relationship map. The corpus callosum forms an arc from the genu through the body and isthmus to the splenium. The rostrum descends beneath the genu. The cingulate gyrus lies above the callosum, with supracallosal cingulum in its white matter.

### Step 1: Separate callosum, anterior commissure and fornix

**Claim:** A sagittal image invites a predictable error: treating every midline white matter structure as the corpus callosum. Compare the CC with the anterior commissure and with the fornix below the body.

### Step 2: Place the SMA and frontal aslant relationship

**Claim:** Above the anterior body, the medial frontal surface brings supplementary and medial premotor parcel territory into the corridor. Compare SCEF, 6ma and 6mp, then place the frontal aslant tract beside that medial frontal relationship without treating any one parcel as the SMA or pre-SMA boundary.

### Step 5: Use segmental topography as a disconnection map

**Claim:** A useful disconnection question asks which callosal segment links which cortical territory. Tractography provides a connectivity model, not a set of sharply bounded individual compartments.

### Step 6: Explain why splenial disconnection can spare motor output

**Claim:** Finish with a relationship, not a prediction. A focus in the posterior portion of the whole CC sample can disturb transfer of visual information between hemispheres while leaving the main descending motor pathway and much of the callosal body outside the primary lesion.
