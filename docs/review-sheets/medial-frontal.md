## Opening question (conference prompt)

A colleague says: “Power is normal, so the motor system is probably not involved.” Which part of that inference can you support, and which relationships would you investigate before accepting it?

## Steps (debrief entries)

### Debrief 0: Describe the distinction before assigning a label

**Claim:** The new observation invites a distinction between initiating or sequencing an action and producing force once an action begins. It does not establish a syndrome. A useful conference response explains why the initial brief power examination did not settle the question.

**Sources:** NO SOURCES

### Debrief 1: Use the medial anatomy as a reference

**Claim:** Published SMA-region glioma cohorts describe postoperative motor and speech changes. They support discussing medial motor systems alongside primary motor and descending pathways. They do not establish the cause of this fictional preoperative finding or predict this individual’s recovery.

**Sources:**
- **sma** — Resection of supplementary motor area gliomas: revisiting supplementary motor syndrome and the role of the frontal aslant tract (PMID 34598138)

### Debrief 2: Keep the alternatives visible

**Claim:** For this exercise, state what additional examination and imaging would help distinguish initiation difficulty from weakness or other causes of slowed performance. Do not turn the schematic separation from area 4 into evidence that subcortical or vascular relationships are preserved.

**Sources:** NO SOURCES

### Debrief 3: A defensible response

**Claim:** “The brief strength examination is reassuring about the task tested, but it does not resolve the reported initiation difficulty. I would describe the medial frontal relationships, examine initiation and sequencing more closely, and keep the deep margin and individual functional organization explicitly unresolved.” This is an example of reasoning, not the only acceptable wording.
