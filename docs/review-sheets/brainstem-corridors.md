# Brainstem & cranial nerve corridors

Lesson id: `brainstem-corridors` · Minutes: 16 · Review status: draft

## Opening question

Left whole-face lower motor neuron facial weakness accompanies right limb weakness. How could left pontine facial nucleus or fascicle involvement together with the left CST explain this pattern, and which trigeminal component and modality would refine the localisation?

## Three takeaways

1. Follow the CST from cerebral crus into the basis pontis with the surface nearly transparent. _(step 0: Read the CST from crus to pons)_
2. Compare MCP, SCP, ICP and DRTT without implying that the absent nuclei are rendered. _(step 2: Compare the cerebellar peduncles and DRTT)_
3. Localise this crossed facial and limb pattern to the left intra-axial pons, involving the facial nucleus or fascicles and the descending CST. _(step 6: Localise a crossed brainstem pattern)_

## Steps

### Step 0: Read the CST from crus to pons

**Claim:** Begin with the descending corticospinal tract. In the anterior view, follow it from the cerebral crus into the basis pontis, using a very low cortical surface opacity so the brainstem relationship is read through the bundle rather than through an absent brainstem mesh.

### Step 4: Place the cranial nerve exits

**Claim:** Read cranial nerve relationships from the displayed trajectories against expected anatomy: CN III in the interpeduncular fossa, CN V at the lateral mid pons at the MCP junction, and CN VII and CN VIII at the pontomedullary junction heading toward the cerebellopontine angle. The displayed samples are overextended; they cannot establish emergence points, isolated cisternal segments, nuclei or fascicles.

### Step 5: Keep safe entry zones as missing anatomy

**Claim:** Cadaveric and tractography studies describe safe entry zones by relating surface landmarks to the long tracts. Make this a missing anatomy exercise: the scene juxtaposes families, but does not render a selected pontine section, a cranial nerve nucleus or fascicle, a perforator, a lesion or patient registration. The sampled CN V course is shown at the lateral mid pons and MCP junction, while ML is a long three-dimensional course rather than a selected pontine slice.

### Step 6: Localise a crossed brainstem pattern

**Claim:** Teaching vignette: on the left, whole-face lower motor neuron facial weakness accompanies right limb weakness. Treat this as a localisation hypothesis for an intra-axial pontine relationship, not as a lesion at the cisternal CN VII exit. A trigeminal sign would need its component and modality specified before it refined the level.
