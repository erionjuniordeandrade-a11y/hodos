# Default mode network · hubs, subsystems & connections

Lesson id: `default-mode-network` · Minutes: 18 · Review status: draft

**Summary:** Orient medial and lateral default-network territories, compare cingulum and medial-temporal relationships, and interpret internal cognition without a “resting brain” shortcut.

**Goals:**
  - Locate medial prefrontal, posterior medial and lateral parietal territories.
  - Distinguish the midline core from medial-temporal and dorsomedial subsystems.
  - Explain the difference between network coupling, white-matter course and cognitive performance.

**Audience:** Neurosurgical residents

## Opening question

Motor examination and conversation seem preserved, but recalling familiar events is difficult. What further anatomical account is needed?

## Three takeaways

1. Orient anterior medial, posterior cingulate and retrosplenial references. _(step 1: Orient the anterior and posterior medial references)_
2. Compare cingulum trajectories with functional coupling without equating them. _(step 2: Compare cingulum anatomy with functional coupling)_
3. Relate medial-temporal anatomy to a specific cognitive operation rather than a broad network label. _(step 3: Separate the medial-temporal subsystem from the wider network)_

## Steps

### Step 0: Find a distributed network across the medial and lateral surfaces

**Claim:** The default mode network includes separated medial prefrontal, posterior medial and lateral parietal territories. Begin by following the red Yeo-7 wash around the surface, rather than looking for one central structure.

**Observe:** Start medially, then use the lateral view to find the parietal component. Return to the medial view and describe which territory lies anterior and which lies posterior.

**Anatomy:**
  - A group network partition identifies cortical territories with related resting-state signals.
  - The wash is categorical membership at one atlas scale. Red does not mean increased activation, metabolic demand or surgical risk.

**Surgical:** A medial frontal or posterior medial lesion can raise cognitive questions beyond the local motor and visual landmarks. Describe its anatomical level first, then specify the cognitive observation to be investigated.

**Question:** Why would a lateral-only image give an incomplete anatomical account of this network?

**Answer:** It underrepresents the medial prefrontal and posterior medial components. A network account needs both medial and lateral relationships, just as a lesion description needs its actual surface and depth.

### Step 1: Orient the anterior and posterior medial references

**Claim:** Medial prefrontal and posterior cingulate/retrosplenial regions form an anterior–posterior relationship around the medial hemisphere. HCP 10r, 31pv and RSC are reference pointers within that broader anatomy.

**Observe:** Alternate between 10r, posterior cingulate 31pv and retrosplenial RSC. Follow the anterior–posterior relationship around the callosal region; the outlines do not reproduce the entire functional midline core.

**Anatomy:**
  - Andrews-Hanna’s study identified anterior medial prefrontal and posterior cingulate regions as a core connecting other default-network components.
  - “Posterior medial cortex” is broader than any one posterior cingulate parcel; gyral, parcel and functional boundaries must remain distinguishable.

**Surgical:** For an interhemispheric or posterior medial case, distinguish the cortical target from the deeper callosal and cingulum relationships. Network terminology should add to that structural account.

**Question:** What does the label “posterior DMN hub” omit from a surgical anatomical description?

**Answer:** Its actual cortical location and extent, side, depth and neighbouring structures. A hub designation describes a role in a network analysis; it does not supply an operative tissue boundary.

### Step 2: Compare cingulum anatomy with functional coupling

**Claim:** Cingulum pathways provide structural context for medial frontal, posterior medial and parahippocampal relationships. Structural trajectories and correlated fMRI signals contribute different kinds of evidence.

**Observe:** Follow the frontal–parietal and parahippocampal–parietal cingulum samples. Compare their course with the posterior medial parcel and hippocampal reference.

**Anatomy:**
  - The cingulum family contains distinct sampled components, not a single line that passes through every default-network region.
  - Greicius combined functional and structural imaging to study medial prefrontal–posterior cingulate and medial temporal–retrosplenial relationships.

**Surgical:** A lesion near the cingulum can motivate a disconnection question even when separated cortical territories are preserved. That question requires actual lesion–pathway anatomy and cognitive observations.

**Question:** If two default-network regions have correlated resting-state signals, must one displayed cingulum streamline connect them directly?

**Answer:** No. Functional coupling and a direct reconstructed connection are different observations. Common inputs and indirect relationships can contribute to correlation, and a displayed tract sample does not enumerate the entire network.

### Step 3: Separate the medial-temporal subsystem from the wider network

**Claim:** The default network has internal structure. One studied component relates medial temporal, retrosplenial and posterior parietal regions; another relates dorsomedial prefrontal and lateral temporal/parietal regions.

**Observe:** Compare hippocampus, posterior cingulate 31pv, retrosplenial RSC and lateral parietal PGp. These orient part of the medial-temporal relationship; the seven-network wash does not subdivide the two systems.

**Anatomy:**
  - In Andrews-Hanna’s experiments, the medial-temporal component was engaged when constructing memory-based scenes.
  - The midline core and the two subsystems were defined by that study’s connectivity and tasks, not by these HCP/subcortical labels.

**Surgical:** Memory-oriented discussion of a medial temporal lesion should consider its connected cortical relationships. A single preserved hippocampal outline cannot summarize memory performance or connected tissue integrity.

**Question:** Why is “DMN impairment” less informative than describing a specific difficulty with memory-based scene construction?

**Answer:** The latter states a cognitive operation to assess and compare. The network label combines several components and tasks; it does not specify which ability changed or which anatomical relationship might explain it.

### Step 4: Understand “default” without calling the brain inactive

**Claim:** The term arose from reproducible changes relative to baseline during tasks. It does not mean the network operates only when a person is doing nothing; internally directed cognition can itself be an active task.

**Observe:** Inspect the same DMN wash while comparing two imagined task instructions: attend to an external target, or construct a personally relevant future event. The colour stays unchanged because this is an atlas.

**Anatomy:**
  - Raichle’s baseline measurements gave meaning to task-related decreases in selected regions.
  - Later experiments linked default-network components to self-relevant and memory-based cognition. A change in task contrast is not equivalent to a brain-wide on/off switch.

**Surgical:** A task-related decrease should not be described as evidence that tissue is functionless. A cognitive account must state the comparison condition and the operation being studied.

**Question:** Does a region showing less signal during an externally directed task become anatomically irrelevant during surgery?

**Answer:** No. Relative signal change answers a question about that task and baseline. It does not establish expendability, and it does not describe all of the region’s functions or connections.

### Step 5: Present a posterior medial case with a testable cognitive question

**Claim:** Teaching vignette: after a posterior medial procedure, conversation and limb strength seem preserved, but the person reports a new difficulty reconstructing familiar events. Build an account that goes beyond a normal motor examination.

**Observe:** Relate posterior medial 31pv to the cingulum and hippocampal reference. Identify the local cortical, connection and broader cognitive questions that require individual evidence.

**Anatomy:**
  - The posterior medial region participates in distributed cognitive relationships, including memory-related systems.
  - Preserved motor performance and a subjective cognitive complaint are different observations; neither defines the structural mechanism of the other.

**Surgical:** Characterize the complaint against baseline and formal cognitive findings, then relate it to the actual lesion and connected anatomy. The atlas provides a hypothesis to examine, not a DMN-specific postoperative diagnosis.

**Question:** What would make this case presentation stronger than “the DMN was injured”?

**Answer:** A description of the new ability or difficulty, its time course and objective assessment, plus the individual cortical and white-matter anatomy. Then explain why a posterior medial or connection-level mechanism is plausible and which alternatives remain.
