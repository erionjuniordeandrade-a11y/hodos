# What the anatomy image can establish

Lesson id: `evidence-classes` · Minutes: 8 · Review status: draft

**Summary:** Use one region to distinguish a surface landmark, an atlas boundary, a reconstruction and a measured functional observation.

**Goals:**
  - Identify the object supporting each statement.
  - Explain why an atlas label and a functional finding answer different questions.

**Audience:** Neurosurgical residents

## Opening question

A highlighted parcel, a nearby tract and weakness appear in one report. What does each actually establish?

## Three takeaways

1. Identify whether the object is a surface landmark, parcel label or reconstruction. _(step 0: A landmark and a parcel answer different questions)_
2. Attach a functional statement to its measurement and method. _(step 2: Put the task beside a functional claim)_
3. Keep atlas anatomy, reconstructed course and examination findings distinguishable. _(step 3: Construct a statement with the correct scope)_

## Steps

### Step 0: A landmark and a parcel answer different questions

**Claim:** A sulcus describes surface shape. A parcel divides cortex according to an atlas method. The two can be anatomically related without sharing the same boundary.

**Observe:** Compare 4 and 3b around the central region. Describe the surface relationship first, then identify what the parcel contour adds.

**Anatomy:**
  - HCP-MMP1 combines several cortical features to define its regions.
  - A parcel name does not replace a description of gyrus, sulcus, side and surface.

**Surgical:** When presenting a lesion, use atlas terminology alongside individual structural anatomy. A template label should not erase a visible anatomical displacement or an uncertain sulcal identification.

**Question:** Does a parcel contour certify the exact functional edge of an individual gyrus?

**Answer:** No. It identifies the atlas boundary on this reference surface. Individual functional localization is a separate observation, and the structural gyrus still needs to be identified on the individual anatomy.

### Step 1: A plausible course is an anatomical hypothesis

**Claim:** The CST sample provides a three-dimensional trajectory to inspect. Its appearance can support a comparison with expected anatomy, but it does not verify every connection.

**Observe:** Follow the descending sample and identify one spatial observation you can make directly. Then name one claim that would need additional evidence.

**Anatomy:**
  - A streamline is a reconstruction from diffusion data, not a histological axon.
  - The tractography validation challenge found plausible-looking false connections under its tested conditions.

**Surgical:** A neat reconstruction should prompt an anatomical consistency check, including its location and method, rather than ending the assessment.

**Question:** Which is directly observed here: a rendered descending course or a verified functioning corticospinal connection?

**Answer:** A rendered descending course. Verifying the latter needs evidence beyond this display. The distinction does not make the image useless: it defines the spatial question the image can help you investigate.

### Step 2: Put the task beside a functional claim

**Claim:** A functional observation is tied to a task, modality and population. Saying “this tract is language” discards the distinctions that make an experiment interpretable.

**Observe:** Inspect the left FAT sample, then compare a verbal-fluency correlation with phrase-boundary disruption during stimulation. Neither experiment is being replayed on this brain.

**Anatomy:**
  - A cohort association relates measured variables; it does not reproduce a stimulation experiment.
  - A stimulation finding is task-specific and localized under the study conditions.

**Surgical:** Use precise task descriptions when discussing function near a lesion. A tract name is a starting point for the question, not a replacement for the clinical observation.

**Question:** Are a fluency correlation and a stimulation-induced planning disruption the same evidence?

**Answer:** No. The first is an association between measurements; the second tests a local perturbation under particular task conditions. Both require their population and method to remain attached to the interpretation.

### Step 3: Construct a statement with the correct scope

**Claim:** Teaching vignette: a report labels a highlighted cortical patch, a nearby tract and a patient’s weakness as one finding. Separate the three observations before interpreting their relationship.

**Observe:** Use the current parcel and pathway to form two separate image descriptions. Add a third sentence stating what an actual clinical examination would contribute.

**Anatomy:**
  - The parcel describes reference identity; the streamlines describe reconstructed geometry.
  - A clinical deficit is an observation about a person. This reference scene contains no such examination.

**Surgical:** A useful case discussion keeps anatomy, reconstruction and function connected in the reasoning while making their sources individually clear.

**Question:** Complete the three-part account: “The atlas shows…; the reconstruction suggests…; the examination establishes…”

**Answer:** The atlas shows a reference parcel; the reconstruction suggests a spatial pathway relationship; the examination establishes the observed clinical deficit. Whether they explain one another is a further question requiring individual localization and supporting evidence.
