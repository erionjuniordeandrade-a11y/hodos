# Insula, central core & interoception

Lesson id: `interoception` · Minutes: 12 · Review status: draft

**Summary:** Orient the buried insular surface and its deep neighbours before considering a proposed interoceptive framework.

**Goals:**
  - Distinguish anterior and posterior insular references.
  - Describe the lateral-to-medial relationship with the central core.
  - Separate visible anatomy from a proposed functional circuit.

**Audience:** Neurosurgical residents

## Opening question

A left insular lesion extends toward the lentiform complex despite preserved naming. Which anatomical and functional observations are still needed?

## Three takeaways

1. Orient the buried insular surface and its opercular relationships. _(step 0: Find the insula beneath the opercula)_
2. Describe the capsule, claustral and lentiform sequence while naming the layers absent from the scene. _(step 2: Place the lentiform complex deeper than insula)_
3. Add projection-pathway and vascular questions when explaining medial extension. _(step 4: Present an insular lesion in anatomical layers)_

## Steps

### Step 0: Find the insula beneath the opercula

**Claim:** The insula forms a buried cortical surface within the Sylvian region. Frontoorbital, frontoparietal and temporal opercula overlie it; seeing an insular parcel requires understanding that depth relationship.

**Observe:** Select AVI and PoI2 in turn with the cortex transparent. They orient anterior ventral and posterior insular regions; the view is a transparency exercise, not an opercular dissection.

**Anatomy:**
  - The central insular sulcus divides an anterior short-gyral region from a posterior long-gyral region.
  - HCP parcels subdivide the insular cortex differently from the gross gyral description.

**Surgical:** A lateral cortical landmark and a buried insular target are at different depths. Describe the overlying opercular relationship before discussing the deeper anatomy.

**Question:** Why does an insular parcel fail to behave like a patch on the exposed lateral convexity?

**Answer:** Because the insula is covered by the opercula. The selected parcel belongs to a buried surface. Transparency may help locate it, but does not reproduce the anatomy or exposure of a surgical dissection.

### Step 1: Orient anterior and posterior insular regions

**Claim:** Gross short and long gyri, atlas parcels and functional descriptions are three different ways to describe insular cortex. Establish anterior and posterior before moving between them.

**Observe:** Compare posterior parcel PoI2 with AVI. Rotate to confirm their relative positions instead of inferring anterior from the screen’s left edge.

**Anatomy:**
  - The central insular sulcus is a gross anatomical divider; the short/long-gyral pattern has individual variation.
  - PoI2 and AVI identify reference parcels, not the outlines of all posterior or anterior gyri.

**Surgical:** An insular-lesion description should specify anterior/posterior and superior/inferior relationships. The single word “insula” hides substantial spatial detail.

**Question:** Does the AVI label outline all the anterior short insular gyri?

**Answer:** No. It identifies an HCP parcel in anterior ventral insula. Gross gyri and multimodal parcels are different subdivisions. Use both descriptions appropriately without treating one as an exact replacement for the other.

### Step 2: Place the lentiform complex deeper than insula

**Claim:** The insular surface is the lateral face of the central core. Deep to it lie capsule and claustral layers before the putamen and pallidum; the internal capsule lies further medially.

**Observe:** Compare the insular reference with putamen and pallidum, then locate thalamus medially. The thin capsule layers and claustrum are not separately modelled here.

**Anatomy:**
  - From lateral to medial: insular cortex, extreme capsule, claustrum, external capsule, putamen, pallidum, then internal capsule.
  - Beyond the internal capsule, the medial neighbour changes with anteroposterior level: caudate head anteriorly and thalamus posteriorly.

**Surgical:** A deeper insular extension brings the lentiform and internal-capsular relationships into the discussion. Projection pathways and their blood supply can account for motor consequences beyond a cortical insular description.

**Question:** Where does the claustrum sit in the sequence from insula to putamen, and which capsule separates it from putamen?

**Answer:** The claustrum lies between the extreme capsule laterally and external capsule medially. The external capsule separates it from putamen. These thin layers are omitted from the render, so use the visible insula and putamen as the outer references for a sequence learned from sectional or dissection anatomy.

### Step 3: Add a functional framework without inventing a circuit

**Claim:** Interoception concerns internal bodily state. Craig’s framework relates posterior and anterior insular processing to bodily representation and feeling; it is a conceptual account, not a recording played by this atlas.

**Observe:** Compare the anterior and posterior reference parcels, then point out which relay nuclei and connecting edges would be needed to render a specific proposed circuit. They are not installed.

**Anatomy:**
  - Gross thalamus cannot substitute for a named relay nucleus such as VMpo.
  - Mesulam and Mufson’s insular output study used tracers in old-world monkeys. That experimental anatomy is distinct from Craig’s conceptual synthesis and does not establish the same circuit in an individual human.
  - A sequence of teaching highlights establishes the order of explanation, not neural timing or direction.

**Surgical:** A symptom involving bodily sensation or awareness can motivate an insular-network question. It does not allow an atlas parcel to diagnose the mechanism of an individual symptom.

**Question:** If the highlight moves from posterior to anterior insula, what has actually moved?

**Answer:** The focus of the explanation. No neural signal has been measured, and no latency, causal sequence or individual connection has been established. The framework and its evidence remain separate from the reference geometry.

### Step 4: Present an insular lesion in anatomical layers

**Claim:** Hypothetical case: a left insular lesion extends toward the lentiform complex. Picture naming is preserved. Build an anatomical account of the surface, depth and neighbouring systems before interpreting the functional observation.

**Observe:** Start with the insular parcel, identify the opercular relationship, then describe the deep lentiform and capsular neighbourhood. State which of those layers are absent from this render.

**Anatomy:**
  - A useful account includes surface, depth and neighbouring systems.
  - A proposed functional framework can complement that account but does not supply missing vessels, nuclei or tissue planes.

**Surgical:** Medial extension raises projection-pathway and lenticulostriate vascular questions. This atlas has no vascular layer, so the relevant vessels and their relation to the lesion cannot be assessed here. Keep that missing anatomy explicit in the layered case description.

**Question:** Which missing anatomical and functional observations could change your account of this insular lesion, despite preserved naming?

**Answer:** Establish the lesion’s relationship to the lentiform complex, internal capsule and relevant vessels on individual imaging. Define the language tasks and baseline performance: preserved picture naming does not characterize every language operation. New motor findings would also raise projection-pathway and vascular questions. The atlas has no vessel layer or individual functional map, so those uncertainties remain unresolved here.
