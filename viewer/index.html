Anatomy, in context.

Hodos is an interactive coursebook for neurosurgical residents. Cortex, white matter pathways and brain networks sit in one anatomical frame, and every lesson asks you to orient the structure, compare its neighbours and explain the relationship before the answer is shown.

Open the atlasStart a lesson

Free, in the browser, no registration. Reference atlas for study, not navigation.

Three moves in every lesson.

Orient

Locate the structures on the reference brain. Parcels, pathways and deep nuclei are named by their atlas codes so the picture and the vocabulary stay together.

Compare

Put the neighbours in the same view. What lies in front, behind and beneath, and which pathway runs between them.

Explain

Answer a resident-level question first, then reveal the explanation with its sources. The aim is anatomy you can articulate, not anatomy you recognise.

Ten lessons, three parts.

From reading the evidence in an image to the networks behind a specific clinical observation. Each lesson is a guided sequence on the live atlas and takes about ten minutes.

Published reference data, cited on every lesson.

Cortical surface of the Human Connectome Project group average.
HCP-MMP1 cortical parcels
Multimodal parcellation; parcels are named by their atlas codes.
HCP1065 sampled tractography
Population-averaged pathways, sampled for teaching. Sparse where the data are sparse.
Melbourne subcortex atlas
Deep nuclei and the central core.

Licences, provenance and the limits of each dataset are on the sources and use terms page. Hodos is an educational reference atlas. It is not patient-specific mapping and not surgical navigation.

Made by a neurosurgeon who teaches residents.

Hodos was built by Dr. Erion de Andrade, neurosurgeon in Porto Alegre with skull base and neuro-oncology fellowships at Cleveland Clinic and Emory University, and residency preceptor at Hospital São José, Santa Casa de Porto Alegre. The name is the Greek word for path, the root of hodotopy: function lives in the pathways as much as in the cortex.
